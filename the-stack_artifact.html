Emergence · Act VIII

A working computer, built up from a rule you could write on a napkin.

No magic, no shortcuts — each rung below stands on the one beneath it, and every claim is checked by running the raw automaton. Two universes, one story: Wireworld (built to compute) and Conway's Life (the barest rule there is). This is the ladder from that rule to a processor that runs real programs.

Wireworld  ·  a wire cell becomes an electron iff 1 or 2 of its neighbours are electrons
Conway's Life  ·  a cell lives iff it has exactly 3 live neighbours, or 2 and is already alive
01

The signal

A moving 1. In Wireworld, an electron running one cell per tick down a wire. In Life, a glider — five cells that crawl diagonally forever — emergent from the rule, not built into it. A glider gun is a source that fires one every 30 ticks.

02

The gate verified in the raw rule

Where signals meet, the rule is logic. In Life, gates are literally glider collisions — two gliders annihilate (that's NOT / inhibit), or merge into one only when both are present (that's AND). From them, NAND — the universal gate.

03

Arithmetic 6 + 5 = 11, verified

XOR beside AND is a half-adder; two make a full-adder; rippling them adds whole numbers. Our own gliders compute 3 + 2 = 5 bit by bit — and the real thing exists at scale: we parsed Nicholas Carlini's 137,712-cell ripple-carry glider adder, ran it 2²⁰ generations on a hashlife cross-checked against our engine, and decoded its output streams — 6 + 5 = 11, entirely in gliders. A live slice of it runs in the viewer.

04

Routing hardware → a routed adder half-adder · full adder · 10-bit

To wire gates together you must turn a signal and fan it out. The Snark reflects a glider 90°; the duplicator splits one in two — both verified cell-by-cell. From them, a routed AND and a routed XOR (its second leg decoupled onto its own right-side duplicator so the deleter is born beside its target — the hard part); side by side they are a half-adder, and two half-adders a full adder. Rippling those computes 10-bit addition — 511+1=512, 682+341=1023 — every bit run through the routed cell in the bare rule. Whole truth tables exact, every catalyst restored to the cell.

05

The computer runs real programs

In Wireworld — the CA built for circuits — the gates compose into a genuine CPU: SAP-1 and the bigger SAP-2 (256 bytes, a stack, ~35 instructions), each one flat netlist of just a NAND gate and a D flip-flop. It runs multiply, divide, GCD, primes, bubble-sort, Fibonacci — watchable live, register by register.

06 · The horizon the honest frontier

A CPU built entirely of physical gliders is a genuine multi-year undertaking (the "Life computer") — but the pieces keep falling. Combinational logic is done: routed AND, routed XOR, a half-adder, a full adder, 10-bit addition. The routing kit is done: fan-out by chained duplicators, Snark turns, a lane-shifter, a crossover. And the first sequential piece now exists too — a 1-bit memory, one glider circulating a 4-Snark loop forever (exactly periodic, proven). What remains is the mountain of timing-closure at scale: thousands of these wired together in lockstep. Nothing here is inflated — the CPU that runs today is the Wireworld one; the glider machine is honest, real, and climbing.

Every gate, adder, reflector, and CPU cell in this ladder is verified by running the cellular automaton itself — source at github.com/Olivesz/emergence. From "3 to be born, 2 to survive" to a machine that computes.
