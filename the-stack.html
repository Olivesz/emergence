01

The signal

A moving 1. In Wireworld, an electron running one cell per tick down a wire. In Life, a glider — five cells that crawl diagonally forever — emergent from the rule, not built into it. A glider gun is a source that fires one every 30 ticks.

02

The gate verified in the raw rule

Where signals meet, the rule is logic. In Life, gates are literally glider collisions — two gliders annihilate (that's NOT / inhibit), or merge into one only when both are present (that's AND). From them, NAND — the universal gate.

03

Arithmetic 6 + 5 = 11, verified

XOR beside AND is a half-adder; two make a full-adder; rippling them adds whole numbers. Our own gliders compute 3 + 2 = 5 bit by bit — and the real thing exists at scale: we parsed Nicholas Carlini's 137,712-cell ripple-carry glider adder, ran it 2²⁰ generations on a hashlife cross-checked against our engine, and decoded its output streams — 6 + 5 = 11, entirely in gliders. A live slice of it runs in the viewer.

04

Routing hardware → a routed half-adder half-adder, run per bit

To wire gates together you must turn a signal and fan it out. The Snark reflects a glider 90°; the duplicator splits one in two — both verified cell-by-cell. From them, a routed AND and a routed XOR (its second leg decoupled onto its own right-side duplicator so the deleter is born beside its target — the hard part); side by side they are a half-adder. Run once per bit, with the Python harness passing sums and carries between runs, that one routed cell adds 10-bit numbers — 511+1=512, 682+341=1023 — every half-adder evaluated in the bare rule. Whole truth tables exact, every catalyst restored to the cell.

05

The computer runs real programs

The gates compose into a genuine CPU: SAP-1 and the bigger SAP-2 (256 bytes, a stack, ~35 instructions), each one flat netlist of just NAND gates and D flip-flops, SAP-2's memory included. It runs multiply, divide, GCD, primes, bubble-sort, Fibonacci — watchable live, register by register. A compiler lays such netlists into raw Wireworld: a full adder, a 4-bit counter and a flip-flop run in the rule itself, matching the netlist on every clock. The whole CPU has not been compiled yet. The glider logic above is its own build, in Conway's Life.

06 · The horizon what is next

A CPU built entirely of physical gliders is a genuine multi-year undertaking (the "Life computer") — but the pieces keep falling. Routed AND, routed XOR and a routed half-adder are done; multi-bit sums so far pass carries between half-adder runs in Python. The routing kit is done: fan-out by chained duplicators, Snark turns, a lane-shifter, a crossover. And the first sequential piece now exists too — a 1-bit memory, one glider circulating a 4-Snark loop forever (exactly periodic, proven). And a compiler has begun: it routes one gate's output straight into another's input (proven composition), which is what hand-building couldn't do. Chasing it turned up a real wall and its way around — a reflector that emits onto a lane also blocks that lane, so two gliders can't be merged onto one wire by mirrors (checked: 340 placements, 0 clean); but fan-in doesn't need a mirror — OR = NOT(AND(NOT a, NOT b)) rebuilds it from the exact reactions already verified, so the whole adder lives in the proven fragment. What remains is the mountain of timing-closure at scale: thousands of these wired together in lockstep. The same problem is the open one in Wireworld: a compiled circuit works only when every gate's inputs arrive on the same 33-tick beat, and the full CPU needs its delays planned into the layout before it can be routed.

Every gate, adder, reflector, and CPU cell in this ladder is verified by running the cellular automaton itself — source at github.com/Olivesz/emergence. From "3 to be born, 2 to survive" to a machine that computes.
